# 02 — Applied precedents and real-world data sources for "embeddings as auxiliary variables"

Status: research note, 2026-10-23. Scope: cases where unstructured data (website text, registration text, job ads, documents, images, phone data) was used as *auxiliary information for estimation* (stratification, nonresponse adjustment, calibration or model-assisted estimation, SAE, imputation). Cases where it was used only to code or classify faster are noted when they show that the text already sits on a frame. The second half is a data-source map for an NSO.

## 0. Bottom line for a skeptic

1. **Unstructured auxiliary data in official estimation is not new.** Image-derived auxiliaries have been in production SAE since the 1980s. Battese–Harter–Fuller (1988) used LANDSAT pixel counts as unit-level covariates. NASS ran that estimator for Midwestern county crop areas in 1982–87. NASS and StatCan use remote sensing in production crop estimates today. The new element is the **generic, task-agnostic embedding**. MOSAIKS, AlphaEarth and PDFM are the image and geo versions of it, and they are now being tested for poverty mapping.
2. **The closest business-survey precedent is ISTAT's ICT-in-enterprises program.** Scraped website text is turned into **task-specific predicted y's**, and those predictions enter model-assisted, calibration and difference estimators. The reported gains are **modest**. In most domains the survey, model and combined estimates were "not significantly different", and the 2018 round needed a modified estimator to handle **selection bias from units without a found website**. No paper I found uses *raw embeddings* as calibration or GREG auxiliaries.
3. **I found no published precedent for text embeddings in stratification or in nonresponse propensity models.** The searches for "embeddings + stratification", "embeddings + nonresponse/propensity" and "LLM + SAE covariates" all came back empty. That gap is both the opportunity and a warning sign.
4. **Coverage is the binding constraint, and it is size-graded.** EU enterprises with 10+ persons had a website at 78.25% in 2023. By size class (2025) the share runs from 76.67% for small to 95.65% for large, with a country spread from over 90% to under 60%. Micro firms are much lower: ABS reported 31% for businesses with fewer than 5 employees in 2008–09, and Mazzoni et al. report 64% for Italian micro firms. For US nonprofits I measured website presence in e-filed returns at about 45% for revenue under $200k and about 88% for revenue over $25M. **Missingness of the auxiliary is strongly correlated with size, and size drives y.** Any design-based test must reproduce that pattern rather than assume full coverage.
5. **Registration free text is the only near-universal business text an NSO holds at design time.** Examples are CRA's "description of major business activity", IRS SS-4 line 17, and the ABN main activity. It is short, confidential (accessible to the NSO), and already used for NAICS/ANZSIC coding. It is the realistic first test case for frame-time embeddings. It is **plain text, so it does not justify a multimodal model**.
6. **Multimodal (PDF/image) embeddings are justified mainly in three places**: (a) financial statements and annual reports, where tables and charts carry the signal (public companies, UK charities and companies, ACNC); (b) rendered website pages and screenshots, where layout reveals e-commerce or booking features; (c) native imagery (satellite, street-level). For registration text, mission statements, 990 program descriptions and job ads, a text-only embedding is sufficient, and a multimodal model adds cost without adding information.
7. **The geospatial-SAE literature has already learned two validation lessons**, both relevant to a new auxiliary. (i) Validating ML poverty maps against *direct survey estimates* is misleading. Under design-based simulation on a pseudo-census, models built on public geodata "perform poorly" and do not beat traditional methods (Corral, Henderson & Segovia 2025). (ii) Estimates for *non-sampled* areas are markedly worse because of informative sampling (Newhouse 2023). Our benchmark should adopt the pseudo-census, design-based protocol from the outset.
8. **For financial y (revenue, expenses, assets), admin tax data will dominate any text covariate.** StatCan has used tax data in place of survey data since the mid-1960s. Text auxiliaries make sense for y that admin data do not carry, such as web/ICT features, innovation, AI use (12.2% of Canadian businesses in Q2 2025, very heterogeneous by industry), program type or service mix, and sustainability practices.

## 1. How unstructured data has entered survey estimation (roles)

| Role | Mechanism | Representative precedents | Design-based guarantee? |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Frame coding (productivity) | text → NAICS/NTEE code → stratification variable | StatCan G-Code/ML on BN "MBA" text; Census SS-4 autocoder and ML; ABS Autocoder; Ma (2021) NTEE | n/a. It changes the stratifier, not the estimator. |
| B. Unit-level auxiliary via predicted y | classifier on web text, trained on the survey sample → ŷ for all frame units → model-assisted, calibration or difference estimator | ISTAT ICT (Barcaroli, Scannapieco, Righi, Bianchi et al.); Beręsewicz et al. (2021) OJA model-assisted calibration | Yes, if the estimator is model-assisted (GREG, model calibration or difference); bias protection regardless of model quality. |
| C. Area-level covariate in SAE | image/phone/text features aggregated to areas → Fay–Herriot / nested-error / EBP / ML | BHF (1988) LANDSAT; NASS CDL; World Bank satellite SAE; mobile phone (Schmid et al. 2017); Google Trends (Porter et al. 2014); Wikipedia (Sheehan et al. 2019) | No. Model-based, so the covariate's measurement error and representativeness matter (Marchetti et al. 2015). |
| D. Model-based replacement of the survey | predict y for all units and aggregate | CBS innovative firms from websites; ZEW/Kinne & Lenz; StatCan model-based crop yields | No. This is where bias risk is highest. |
| E. Imputation / mass imputation | text or LLM features → impute item or unit nonresponse | NCSES BERD uses company websites and 10-Ks for substitution; Bank of England LLM hidden states; Kim & Lee (2023) | Depends on the MAR assumption plus the model. |
| F. Hybrid human+model estimation | ML/LLM prediction for all units + labelled probability subsample → PPI = difference estimator | Ye & Yoganarasimhan (2026); PPI-IPW (2025); Dagdoug & Haziza (2026) | Yes (difference/GREG form). |

The user's proposal ("embeddings as covariates for stratification, nonresponse, calibration, SAE") sits mostly in B, C and F. **B with raw embeddings, stratification on embedding clusters, and embedding-based response propensity have no direct published precedent that I could find.**

## 2. Precedents in enterprise and business statistics

### 2.1 ISTAT — ICT usage and e-commerce in enterprises (the anchor precedent)

- **Barcaroli, G., Nurra, A., Salamone, S., Scannapieco, M., Scarnò, M., Summa, D. (2015).** "Internet as Data Source in the Istat Survey on ICT in Enterprises." *Austrian Journal of Statistics* 44(2), 31–43. https://www.ajs.or.at/index.php/ajs/article/view/vol44-2-3
  - Population: enterprises with 10+ persons employed in industry and services. Pilot: about 8,600 websites from about 19,000 responding enterprises. The target was "systematic scraping of the near 100,000 websites" of the whole population.
- **Barcaroli, G., Scannapieco, M., Summa, D. (2016).** "On the use of internet as a data source for official statistics: a strategy for identifying enterprises on the web." *Rivista Italiana di Economia Demografia e Statistica* (RIEDS) 2016. https://www.istat.it/it/files/2018/06/a4_RIEDS-2016.pdf
  - Coverage arithmetic: about 200,000 enterprises, about 70% with a website (about 130,000 sites), about 105,000 URLs identified, so URL coverage is about 81%.
- **ISTAT paper to DGINS 2018**, "ICT survey and web scraped data from enterprise websites" (author not confirmed). https://dgins2018.statisticsevents.ro/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/04-IT-data-integration-challenges-DGINS-paper-Istat_Italy.pdf
  - 2017 figures: about 183,000 enterprises, about 75% with a website (about 135,000). URLs were found for about 101,000, or 75% of the estimated total: 14k from the current survey, 28k from automated URL retrieval, and 59k from earlier rounds and other sources.
- **Barcaroli, G., Bianchi, G., et al. (2016).** "Machine learning and statistical inference: the case of Istat survey on ICT." Italian Statistical Society conference. https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/901e286e98d905768317a3bde45d549754d6b643
- **Barcaroli, G., Scannapieco, M. (2019).** "Integration of ICT survey data and Internet data from enterprises websites at the Italian National Institute of Statistics." *Statistical Journal of the IAOS*. DOI 10.3233/SJI-190553. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/SJI-190553
  - The survey sample is the **training set**. Models are fitted where both survey answers and web-derived terms exist, then applied to every scraped website.
- **Bianchi, G., Barcaroli, G., Righi, P., Rinaldi, M. (2019).** "Producing contingency table estimates integrating survey data and Big Data." ITACOSM 2019 slides. https://www.istat.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/ITACOSM2019-ICT-Righi.pdf
  - 2017 data, about 21,000 respondents, 66% response rate. A **model-assisted calibration estimator** for counts of enterprises and persons employed by domain.
- **ISTAT Technical Report (ICT 2018).** https://www.istat.it/it/files//2020/05/Tech_Report_ICT2018.pdf
  - The production estimator is a calibration estimator on enterprise counts and employment by activity × size class × region. The report introduces **unbiased difference estimators** that use web-predicted values, with sales through web channels as one target.
- **ISTAT experimental statistic: "Estimates of modalities of use of websites by enterprises. Year 2018"** (published 8 May 2020). https://www.istat.it/en/experimental-statistic/estimates-of-modalities-of-use-of-websites-by-enterprises-year-2018/
  - Pipeline: URL acquisition → linkage to the SBR → scraping, text mining and ML → inference from scraped enterprises to the target population. The 2018 edition used **"a modified estimator to reduce the selection bias related to the Big Data source"** compared with 2017.
- Righi, P., Barcaroli, G., Golini, N. (2018). Quality evaluation of experimental big-data statistics, European Conference on Quality in Official Statistics (Q2018), Kraków. Title and text were not retrieved; existence comes from reference lists only.

**What carries over to our project.** (i) Targets were **web-observable y**: web ordering, job-vacancy advertising, social-media links. (ii) Auxiliary = **task-specific predicted y** (one classifier per target), not a generic embedding. (iii) Estimator = calibration, model-assisted or difference, so bias protection holds. (iv) Combined estimates were mostly not significantly different from survey-only estimates. Precision gains were real but small, and no headline relative-efficiency figure appeared in what I could retrieve. (v) The **unit-without-URL stratum** is the main bias source, and it required an explicit fix.

### 2.2 Eurostat — ESSnet Big Data I/II → Web Intelligence Network → Web Intelligence Hub (OBEC)

- ESSnet Big Data I (2016–2018) had WP2, "Web scraping of enterprise web sites", with six principal use cases. ESSnet Big Data II had WPC, "Enterprise Characteristics", and produced a **reference methodological framework for OBEC (Deliverable C6)**. Source: CROS portal pages https://cros.ec.europa.eu/book-page/issue-08-moving-big-data-statistical-production and https://cros.ec.europa.eu/node/523
- **ESSnet Trusted Smart Statistics – Web Intelligence Network (WIN)** ran April 2021–March 2025, with 17 organisations from 14 countries, led by Statistics Poland. WP2 use cases are OJA (online job ads) and **OBEC (Online Based Enterprise Characteristics)**. https://cros.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2023-12/d1.1_wiser_group_inception_report.pdf ; https://cros.ec.europa.eu/book-page/web-intelligence-hub
- WIN, "URL finding methodology" (31 Jan 2022), joint work of WP2 and WP3 UC5. It covers search-engine queries, page scraping, feature extraction and a classifier for "is this the enterprise's site". It argues that scraped sites can supplement the ICT survey, which "struggles with low response rates". https://cros.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2023-12/20220131_url_finding_methodology.pdf
- The OBEC population is enterprises with 10+ employees. Statistics Poland used online business registers to look up URLs. Production status of OBEC in the WIH was not confirmed; the sources say "implementation of the production process is on the way".

**Relevance.** This is the institutional template for "web text for every frame unit". Its documented hard problems (URL finding, linkage, coverage) apply directly to us, before embeddings even enter.

### 2.3 Statistics Netherlands (CBS) and ZEW — innovation from websites (Role D)

- **Daas, P.J.H., van der Doef, S. (2020).** "Detecting innovative companies via their website." *Statistical Journal of the IAOS* 36(4), 1239–1251. DOI 10.3233/SJI-200627. Working-paper version: CBS CBDS WP 01-21, "Using website texts to detect innovative companies". https://www.cbs.nl/-/media/innovatie/using-website-texts-todetect-innovative-companies.pdf
  - Model trained on website text of firms in the Dutch CIS (10+ persons). It reproduced CIS results and detected innovative firms **below 10 employees** (start-ups). A later summary reports about 93% maximum accuracy against survey results.
  - Self-reported issues: **model stability, model bias, minimum number of words extracted, companies without a website**.
  - Later a CBS beta product ("experimental statistics") on innovation in about 500,000 SMEs: https://unstats.un.org/bigdata/events/2024/transport-seminar/presentations/Session%202%20-%20Netherlands%20-%20Web%20scraping%20for%20business%20statistics.pdf
- CBS × Dataprovider (2019): a file of all Dutch corporate websites linked to the General Business Register (ABR) found about 50,000 companies with mostly online turnover. https://www.cbs.nl/en-gb/corporate/2019/20/cbs-and-dataprovider-successfully-map-internet-economy
- **Kinne, J., Lenz, D. (2021).** "Predicting innovative firms using web mining and deep learning." *PLoS ONE* 16(4): e0249071. DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0249071. Labels come from the Mannheim Innovation Panel; a DNN on website text predicts product-innovator status.
- **Kinne, J., Axenbeck, J. (2018).** "Web mining of firm websites: a framework for web scraping and a pilot study for Germany." ZEW Discussion Paper 18-033. https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/46518 . Website use and characteristics differ by firm size, age, location and sector, and low broadband seems to keep some firms offline.

**Relevance.** These show the predictive signal exists for innovation-type y. They are model-based replacements, though, so they do not show design-based variance reduction. They also make the selection-on-website-presence problem explicit.

### 2.4 Business-register and registration text (Role A, but proves frame-time availability)

- **Statistics Canada.** Oyarzun, J. (2018). "The Imitation Game: An Overview of a Machine Learning Approach to Code the Industrial Classification." StatCan International Methodology Symposium 2018, session 3B. https://www.statcan.gc.ca/en/media/2296 . It uses text mining and ML to code missing industrial classifications on the BR.
- **UNECE (2024)**, Canada SBR classification presentation: "NAICS codes are assigned to MBA [major business activity] descriptive text collected through the business registration process using an Automated Classification System". Automated coding uses **G-Code**. A decision-tree tool built with CRA guides registrants. https://unece.org/sites/default/files/2024-10/3.4_Canada%20SBR%20classification_0.pdf
- **CRA, "Gather your information"** (Business Number registration) asks for "a description of major business activity" and "main products or services with estimated percentage of revenue". https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/business-registration/business-number-program-account/gather-information.html . The same registration opens GST/HST, payroll and import/export program accounts. The older RC1C example: "Construction – Installing residential hardwood flooring", described with "at least one noun, a verb, and an adjective". https://www.cchwebsites.com/content/pdf/tax_forms/ca/en/rc1c_en.pdf
- StatCan BR documentation: for new businesses, primary industrial coding is first done by automated coding software, and NAICS codes "may not be up-to-date or can be in error". https://www23.statcan.gc.ca/imdb-bmdi/pub/1105-eng.htm
- **US Census Bureau.** The Business Formation Statistics methodology says an auto-coder uses business name plus EIN-application descriptions and assigned NAICS to **over 80% of incoming applications in 2020**. A Census ML algorithm codes the remainder. The SSA Autocoder works on SS-4 open text. **BEACON** is trained on Economic Census and SS-4 self-descriptions. https://census.gov/econ/bfs/pdf/pr_methodology.pdf ; https://www2.census.gov/library/working-papers/2024/econ/implementing-interactive-classification-tools-2022-economic-census.pdf
- **IRS Form SS-4**: line 16 asks the applicant to check one principal-activity box; line 17 says "Indicate principal line of merchandise sold, specific construction work done, products produced, or services provided." The wording is stable across the Dec 2019, Dec 2023 and Dec 2025 revisions. https://irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/fss4.pdf
- **Cuffe, J., Bhattacharjee, S., Etudo, U., Smith, J., Basdeo, N., Burbank, N., Roberts, S.** "Using Public Data to Generate Industrial Classification Codes." In *Big Data for Twenty-First-Century Economic Statistics* (NBER/U. Chicago Press; 2019 chapter, 2022 volume). https://www.nber.org/chapters/c14278 . Website text plus Google Places reviews for about 120,000 single-unit establishments predicted 2-digit NAICS in **about 59%** of cases. The authors flag "substantial methodological and possible privacy issues". Follow-up: Bhattacharjee, Etudo, Smith, FedCASIC 2021, https://census.gov/fedcasic/fc2021/pdf/6B_Bhattacharjee.pdf
- **ABS.** Methodological News, June 2008: an Autocoder built with the ATO does exact-word matching on ABN registration main-activity text and coded about half of new registrations at about 97% quality (2007). https://www.abs.gov.au/AUSSTATS/abs@.nsf/Lookup/1504.0Main+Features6Jun+2008 . Later, registrants self-code via the ABS-hosted Industry Point of Contact Coder.
- **Hoberg, G., Phillips, G. (2016).** "Text-Based Network Industries and Endogenous Product Differentiation." *Journal of Political Economy* 124(5), 1423–1465. Uses 10-K product descriptions from 50,673 firm-years and is the canonical "business-description similarity" precedent. https://hobergphillips.tuck.dartmouth.edu/tnic_basedata.html
- **Ma, J. (2021).** "Automated coding using machine learning and remapping the U.S. nonprofit sector: A guide and benchmark." *Nonprofit and Voluntary Sector Quarterly* (online 30 Oct 2020). DOI 10.1177/0899764020968153. 90% accuracy for 9 broad NTEE categories and 88% for 25 major groups, recoding more than 439,000 nonprofits from 990 text. Code: https://github.com/ma-ji/npo_classifier

**Relevance.** This settles the availability question: **every new Canadian or US business arrives with a short self-description, and the NSO already holds it.** No one has published using an *embedding* of that text as a continuous auxiliary in estimation, as opposed to collapsing it to a code. That is a precise, novel and realistic test.

### 2.5 Other business-statistics uses of web or online data

- **Statistics Canada, ASML web-scraping pilot**, ISI WSC 2019 STS proceedings, vol. 2, p. 282. The Annual Survey of Manufacturing and Logging prefills questionnaires. The pilot scrapes websites "to better capture the commodities companies make and possibly improve sales estimates", aimed partly at **nonresponse**. https://isi2019.org/proceeding/2.STS/STS%20VOL%202/files/basic-html/page282.html
- **StatCan web scraping policy**: notify companies, use an API in place of scraping where possible, do not scrape personal information, aim to reduce burden. https://www.statcan.gc.ca/en/node/33628
- **NCSES BERD**: item nonresponse for large companies is imputed by "direct substitution of available company data (e.g., data from the company's website, annual Form 10-K report, or administrative sources)". This is web or document data in production imputation. https://ncses.nsf.gov/surveys/business-enterprise-research-development/2023
- **Beręsewicz, M.E., Białkowska, G., Marcinkowski, K., Maślak, M., Opiela, P., Pawlukiewicz, K., Pater, R. (2021).** "Enhancing the Demand for Labour survey by including skills from online job advertisements using model-assisted calibration." *Survey Research Methods* 15(2), 147–167. DOI 10.18148/srm/2021.v15i2.7670. Calibration plus LASSO-assisted calibration corrects coverage and selection error in OJA data against survey totals. Online ads overstate interpersonal and managerial skills because craft and operator occupations are under-represented. This is the closest published precedent for *calibration with high-dimensional text-derived features*.
- Beręsewicz et al., "Estimating the number of entities with vacancies using administrative and online data", arXiv 2106.03263. Dual-system estimation suggests the Polish vacancy survey undercounts entities with vacancies by about 10–15%.
- **Meireles, Pitta, Silva (2023)**, "Web scraping as a source for producing e-commerce indicators: findings from a pilot in Brazil", ISI WSC 2023, Ottawa (Cetic.br). **Negative result**: scraping "failed to produce e-commerce prevalence estimates matching" the survey. The authors now propose combining survey and scraped data for sampled firms. https://isi-web.org/sites/default/files/import//proceedings/CPS-Paper_800_web-scraping-as-a-source-for-producing-e-commerce-indicators-findings-from-a-pilot-in-brazil.pdf
- **Glaeser, E., Kim, H., Luca, M.** "Nowcasting the Local Economy: Using Yelp Data to Measure Economic Activity." NBER WP 24010 (2017); chapter in *Big Data for Twenty-First-Century Economic Statistics*. Yelp counts explain 29.2% of residual variance after lagged CBP, and accuracy is **higher in denser, wealthier, more educated ZIPs**. That is a heterogeneity warning for SAE. https://www.nber.org/papers/w24010

## 3. Precedents in area-level / SAE with images, phones and text

### 3.1 Remote sensing in official SAE (production)
- **Battese, G.E., Harter, R.M., Fuller, W.A. (1988).** "An error-components model for prediction of county crop areas using survey and satellite data." *JASA* 83(401), 28–36. DOI 10.2307/2288915. Twelve Iowa counties, the 1978 June Enumerative Survey, and LANDSAT pixel classifications (about 0.45 ha per pixel). NASS used the Battese–Fuller estimator operationally in 1982–87 for county crop areas. https://nass.usda.gov/Education_and_Outreach/Reports,_Presentations_and_Conferences/GIS_Reports/Application%20of%20Satellite%20Data%20to%20the%20Crop%20Area%20Estimation%20at%20the%20County%20Level.pdf
- **Erciulescu, A.L., Cruze, N.B., Nandram, B. (2019).** "Model-based county level crop estimates incorporating auxiliary sources of information." *JRSS-A* 182(1), 283–303. Hierarchical Bayes subarea model with remote-sensing, weather and FSA administrative covariates, benchmarked to state. Also Cruze et al. (2019), "Producing Official County-Level Agricultural Estimates in the United States", *Statistical Science* 34(2), DOI 10.1214/18-STS687. A 2020 NASS JSM paper (Bellow) compares the Cropland Data Layer with FSA acreage as area-level covariates. https://ww2.amstat.org/meetings/proceedings/2020/data/assets/pdf/1505439.pdf
- **Statistics Canada, model-based principal field crop estimates**: coarse-resolution satellite data (Crop Condition Assessment Program, NDVI) plus agroclimatic data plus the Field Crop Reporting Series. In use since 2016; replaced the July survey yield estimates from 2020. https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/250917/dq250917b-eng.htm

### 3.2 Poverty mapping with satellites (World Bank / academic)
- **Jean, N., Burke, M., Xie, M., Davis, W.M., Lobell, D.B., Ermon, S. (2016).** "Combining satellite imagery and machine learning to predict poverty." *Science* 353(6301), 790–794. DOI 10.1126/science.aaf7894. A CNN on imagery explains up to 75% of variation in local outcomes in five African countries.
- **Engstrom, R., Hersh, J., Newhouse, D. (2022).** "Poverty from Space: Using High-Resolution Satellite Imagery for Estimating Economic Well-Being." *World Bank Economic Review* 36(2), 382–412 (PRWP 8284, 2017). Sri Lanka, 1,291 GN divisions. Features explain 49–61% of variation in mean log consumption and 37–62% for poverty rates; night lights explain only about 15%. https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/05c87b75-ecc1-4221-9e41-a11cf118aba1
- **Newhouse, D. (2023).** "Small Area Estimation of Poverty and Wealth Using Geospatial Data: What Have We Learned So Far?" WB PRWP 10512. DOI 10.1596/1813-9450-10512. Satellite and phone data predict well against census data. Tree-based ML beats linear mixed models. **Sampled areas are significantly more accurate than non-sampled areas because of informative sampling.**
- **Merfeld, J.D., Newhouse, D. (2023).** "Improving Estimates of Mean Welfare and Uncertainty in Developing Countries." WB PRWP 10348. DOI 10.1596/1813-9450-10348. EBP versus Cubist, XGBoost and boosted forests across 7 countries. A two-stage residual bootstrap gives better coverage than the parametric EBP bootstrap but slightly underestimates interval width.
- **Corral, P., Henderson, H., Segovia, S. (2025).** "Poverty mapping in the age of machine learning." *Journal of Development Economics* 172 (PRWP 10429, 2023). Design-based simulation on a pseudo-census from the Mexico 2015 Intercensal Survey. **Standard ML validation against direct estimates is misleading. The public-geodata model "performs poorly against true poverty rates" and does not beat traditional methods in targeting simulations.**
- **Corral, P., Himelein, K., McGee, K., Molina, I. (2021).** "A Map of the Poor or a Poor Map?" *Mathematics* 9(21), 2780. DOI 10.3390/math9212780 (PRWP 9620). Model-based and design-based validation on the Mexican Intercensal as pseudo-census. All SAE methods beat direct estimates in MSE, and transformation matters.
- **Corral, P., Molina, I., Cojocaru, A., Segovia, S. (2022).** *Guidelines to Small Area Estimation for Poverty Mapping.* World Bank, CC BY 3.0 IGO. https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/1d1fcadc-43e3-541b-8949-fea45dd2a528/content (the specific geospatial recommendations were not retrieved).
- **ADB (2020)**, *Mapping Poverty through Data Integration and Artificial Intelligence* (Key Indicators 2020 supplement), and 2021 feasibility studies with PSA (Philippines) and NSO Thailand, with World Data Lab. https://www.adb.org/publications/mapping-poverty-data-integration-ai

### 3.3 Phones, search, Wikipedia, street imagery
- **Blumenstock, J., Cadamuro, G., On, R. (2015).** "Predicting poverty and wealth from mobile phone metadata." *Science* 350(6264), 1073–1076. DOI 10.1126/science.aac4420.
- **Steele, J.E. et al. (2017).** "Mapping poverty using mobile phone and satellite data." *J. R. Soc. Interface* 14: 20160690. Combined data reach r² up to 0.78.
- **Schmid, T., Bruckschen, F., Salvati, N., Zbiranski, T. (2017).** "Constructing sociodemographic indicators for national statistical institutes by using mobile phone data: estimating literacy rates in Senegal." *JRSS-A* 180(4). DOI 10.1111/rssa.12305. SAE written for NSIs, with a design-based simulation.
- **Marchetti, S., Giusti, C., Pratesi, M., Salvati, N., Giannotti, F., Pedreschi, D., Rinzivillo, S., Pappalardo, L., Gabrielli, L. (2015).** "Small Area Model-Based Estimators Using Big Data Sources." *JOS* 31(2), 263–281. DOI 10.1515/JOS-2015-0017. A Fay–Herriot model with a GPS mobility-diversity covariate. Warns of **measurement error and representativeness of big-data covariates**.
- **Porter, A.T., Holan, S.H., Wikle, C.K., Cressie, N. (2014).** "Spatial Fay–Herriot models for small area estimation with functional covariates." *Spatial Statistics* 10, 27–42. DOI 10.1016/j.spasta.2014.07.001. Google Trends curves enter as functional covariates through Karhunen–Loève dimension reduction plus SSVS. **This is the structural template for high-dimensional covariates such as embeddings in Fay–Herriot.**
- **Sheehan, E., Meng, C., Tan, M., Uzkent, B., Jean, N., Burke, M., Lobell, D., Ermon, S. (2019).** "Predicting Economic Development using Geolocated Wikipedia Articles." KDD 2019, 2698–2706. arXiv 1905.01627. Embeddings of nearby Wikipedia articles combined with night lights beat earlier benchmarks. **This is the earliest text-embedding-as-area-covariate precedent I found.**
- **Gebru, T. et al. (2017).** "Using deep learning and Google Street View to estimate the demographic makeup of neighborhoods across the United States." *PNAS*. DOI 10.1073/pnas.1700035114. Vehicle classes from about 50M images in 200 cities, related to ACS at ZIP and precinct level.

### 3.4 The embedding era (2021–2026): task-agnostic representations as covariates
- **Rolf, E., Proctor, J., Carleton, T., Bolliger, I., Shankar, V., Ishihara, M., Recht, B., Hsiang, S. (2021).** "A generalizable and accessible approach to machine learning with global satellite imagery" (MOSAIKS). *Nature Communications* 12, 4392. DOI 10.1038/s41467-021-24638-z. One unsupervised random-feature encoding is computed once and reused across many outcomes. **This is the conceptual twin of "embed once, use as auxiliary for many y's".**
- **AlphaEarth Foundations** (Google/DeepMind, 2025). arXiv 2507.22291. An annual 64-dimensional unit-norm embedding per 10 m pixel, 2017 onward, fusing Sentinel-2, Landsat, SAR, elevation, climate and text sources. About 24% average error reduction against the next-best featurization. Earth Engine dataset `GOOGLE_SATELLITE_EMBEDDING_V1_ANNUAL`; bucket `gs://alphaearth_foundations`. **Sandbox check: `https://storage.googleapis.com/alphaearth_foundations/` returns HTTP 200 and lists `satellite_embedding/v1/annual/{year}/{UTM zone}/*.tiff` (about 3.5 GB COG tiles).** Applications to poverty: arXiv 2511.01408 (AlphaEarth plus GNN, DHS cluster wealth, with "fuzzy label" loss for displaced coordinates); arXiv 2604.23166 (satellite foundation model for wealth monitoring); arXiv 2606.20697 (AEF-Econ). These came from titles and snippets only; their results were not read.
- **Agarwal, M., Prasad, G., et al. (2024–2026).** "General Geospatial Inference with a Population Dynamics Foundation Model." arXiv 2411.07207 (v6, Apr 2026). A GNN over search trends, mobility/busyness, weather and air quality gives **embeddings for every US county and ZCTA**. It reports state of the art on 27 interpolation tasks, per a secondary summary. Access is by request for academic and non-commercial use; all authors are Alphabet employees. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07207
- **Buckmann, M., Nguyen, Q.A., Hill, E. (2025).** "Revealing economic facts: LLMs know more than they say." Bank of England Staff WP 1150, arXiv 2505.08662. A linear model on LLM **hidden states for an entity name** beats the LLM's own text answers for county unemployment and firm total assets. A few dozen labels often suffice, and the method helps imputation and super-resolution. **This is the most direct evidence that a generic embedding of a unit's name or description carries predictive signal for an official-statistics y.**
- **Kim, J., Lee, B. (2023–2024).** "AI-Augmented Surveys: Leveraging Large Language Models and Surveys for Opinion Prediction." arXiv 2305.09620. GSS 1972–2021, 68,846 respondents, 3,110 opinions. Retrodiction AUC 0.86 and public-opinion correlation 0.98; unasked opinions AUC 0.73 and correlation 0.67.

### 3.6 Explicit gaps (searched, nothing found)
- Text or LLM embeddings as **stratification** variables, or embedding-cluster strata, in any survey.
- Text embeddings in **response-propensity / nonresponse weighting** for business or establishment surveys. The only nearby cases are web data in *imputation* (BERD) and ASML web scraping aimed at nonresponse.
- LLM-embedding covariates in **Fay–Herriot / EBLUP** for official SAE. The precedents stop at Wikipedia embeddings (Sheehan 2019), PDFM and AlphaEarth area embeddings, and Google Trends functional covariates.
- Any NSO (StatCan, ONS, ABS, Census, CBS, ISTAT) publication from 2023–2026 that uses *generic* embeddings for weighting, calibration or SAE. ONS Data Science Campus work on SIC-from-websites was not found either.

## 4. Red-team lessons distilled from the precedents

| Lesson | Evidence | Implication for our simulation |
|---|---|---|
| The auxiliary is missing for small units, and smallness predicts y | Eurostat 2025: 76.67% small vs 95.65% large; Italy micro 64% vs big 83%; ABS 2008–09: 31% (<5 employees) vs 95% (200+); our 990 check: about 45% vs 88% | Simulate **realistic, size-correlated missingness** of web text. Report gains separately for the "has text" and "no text" strata. The estimator must handle a missing auxiliary, for example with a missingness indicator or a separate calibration group. |
| URL linkage is imperfect | ISTAT: 75–81% of existing websites found; WIN URL-finding methodology | Add **linkage error** (wrong-site text) as a stress scenario. |
| Gains concentrate on web-observable y | ISTAT targets are website functionalities; Brazil e-commerce mismatch; CBS innovation | Pick y's where text *should* help (program type, service mix) **and** y's where it should not (revenue given size), as a negative control. |
| Validating against direct estimates misleads | Corral, Henderson & Segovia (2025) | Use a **pseudo-census + repeated sampling** protocol only, with truth known for all units. |
| Non-sampled areas are worse | Newhouse (2023) | For SAE, report sampled and non-sampled domains separately. |
| Covariate measurement error and representativeness | Marchetti et al. (2015); Yelp accuracy higher in rich ZIPs | Check whether embedding-model error is heterogeneous across domains, which would create domain-level bias in SAE. |
| Selection bias needs a dedicated estimator fix | ISTAT 2018 "modified estimator" | Compare HT, GREG, model calibration and cross-fitted ML-assisted estimators. Never use pure model-based aggregation as the "embedding" arm without a design-consistent comparator. |
| Strong conventional auxiliaries already exist | StatCan tax replacement since the 1960s; tax data as auxiliary in the Monthly Food Services Survey | The baseline must include **admin financials when they exist at design time** (e.g. prior-year revenue), or the gains will be inflated. |
| High-dimensional p | Goga (2025); Caughey & Hartman (2017); Porter et al. (2014) | Do not put 1,536 raw dimensions into GREG calibration. Use (a) model calibration on a cross-fitted ŷ(embedding), (b) PCA or PLS to k ≤ 10–20, or (c) ridge/lasso-GREG. Report the weight distribution (negative weights, CV of weights). |

## 6. Implications for the setup (for the benchmark-design note)

1. **Realistic test 1 (frame-time text):** embed prior-year 990 mission plus program text, or BN-style short self-descriptions. Use them for **stratification and model calibration** in the current-year survey. Truth = current-year 990 values. This mirrors CRA BN/T3010 text held at StatCan. Embedding *current-year* Schedule O alongside current-year y would be leakage, because Schedule O can quote financial amounts.
2. **Realistic test 2 (est-time web text):** website text available only for linked units, with size-graded missingness and linkage error, as in ISTAT and OBEC. Requires crawling, which the sandbox mostly cannot do. The XML website field supports simulating the missingness pattern even without content.
3. **Baselines** must include NTEE×state×revenue-class stratification, GREG on prior-year revenue and assets, and a "TF-IDF/LSA ŷ" arm. The question is whether *generic* embeddings beat *cheap* text features, not only whether they beat no text.
4. **Estimators:** HT, Hájek, post-stratified, GREG (conventional x), model calibration (Wu–Sitter) on cross-fitted ŷ from embeddings (An–Haziza–Dagdoug–Tillé), ridge-GREG on k PCs, and PPI/difference. Pure model-based prediction is reported only as a contrast.
5. **Multimodal (Cohere embed-v4)** is worth testing only with a PDF-native population. The UK Charity Commission accounts PDFs and Companies House accounts are the two real candidates, but neither is reachable from the sandbox. Third-party listings give embed-v4 as text+image interleaved input, 128k context, output dimensions 256/512/1024/1536, and about $0.12 per 1M input tokens (Bedrock). Cohere's own docs were not reached.
