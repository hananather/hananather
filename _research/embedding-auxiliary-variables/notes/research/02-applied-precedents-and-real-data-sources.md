## 0. Bottom line for a skeptic

1. **Unstructured auxiliary data in official estimation is not new.** Image-derived auxiliaries have been in production SAE since the 1980s. Battese–Harter–Fuller (1988) used LANDSAT pixel counts as unit-level covariates. NASS ran that estimator for Midwestern county crop areas in 1982–87. [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked] NASS and StatCan use remote sensing in production crop estimates today. The new element is the **generic, task-agnostic embedding**. MOSAIKS, AlphaEarth and PDFM are the image and geo versions of it, and they are now being tested for poverty mapping.
2. **The closest business-survey precedent is ISTAT's ICT-in-enterprises program.** Scraped website text is turned into **task-specific predicted y's**, and those predictions enter model-assisted, calibration and difference estimators. The reported gains are **modest**. In most domains the survey, model and combined estimates were "not significantly different", and the 2018 round needed a modified estimator to handle **selection bias from units without a found website**. No paper I found uses *raw embeddings* as calibration or GREG auxiliaries.
3. **I found no published precedent for text embeddings in stratification or in nonresponse propensity models.** The searches for "embeddings + stratification", "embeddings + nonresponse/propensity" and "LLM + SAE covariates" all came back empty. That gap is both the opportunity and a warning sign.
4. **Coverage is the binding constraint, and it is size-graded.** EU enterprises with 10+ persons had a website at 78.25% in 2023. By size class (2025) the share runs from 76.67% for small to 95.65% for large, with a country spread from over 90% to under 60% [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked]. Micro firms are much lower: ABS reported 31% for businesses with fewer than 5 employees in 2008–09 [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked], and Mazzoni et al. report 64% for Italian micro firms [UNVERIFIED: no full citation for Mazzoni et al. is given anywhere in this note]. For US nonprofits I measured website presence in e-filed returns at about 45% for revenue under $200k and about 88% for revenue over $25M (recomputed in the verification pass: CONFIRMED). **Missingness of the auxiliary is strongly correlated with size, and size drives y.** Any design-based test must reproduce that pattern rather than assume full coverage.
5. **Registration free text is the only near-universal business text an NSO holds at design time.** Examples are CRA's "description of major business activity", IRS SS-4 line 17, and the ABN main activity. It is short, confidential (accessible to the NSO), and already used for NAICS/ANZSIC coding. It is the realistic first test case for frame-time embeddings. It is **plain text, so it does not justify a multimodal model**.
6. **Multimodal (PDF/image) embeddings are justified mainly in three places**: (a) financial statements and annual reports, where tables and charts carry the signal (public companies, UK charities and companies, ACNC); (b) rendered website pages and screenshots, where layout reveals e-commerce or booking features; (c) native imagery (satellite, street-level). For registration text, mission statements, 990 program descriptions and job ads, a text-only embedding is sufficient, and a multimodal model adds cost without adding information.
7. **The geospatial-SAE literature has already learned two validation lessons**, both relevant to a new auxiliary. (i) Validating ML poverty maps against *direct survey estimates* is misleading. Under design-based simulation on a pseudo-census, models built on public geodata "perform poorly" and do not beat traditional methods (Corral, Henderson & Segovia 2025). (ii) Estimates for *non-sampled* areas are markedly worse because of informative sampling (Newhouse 2023). Our benchmark should adopt the pseudo-census, design-based protocol from the outset.
8. **For financial y (revenue, expenses, assets), admin tax data will dominate any text covariate.** StatCan has used tax data in place of survey data since the mid-1960s. [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked] Text auxiliaries make sense for y that admin data do not carry, such as web/ICT features, innovation, AI use (12.2% of Canadian businesses in Q2 2025 [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked], very heterogeneous by industry), program type or service mix, and sustainability practices.

## 1. How unstructured data has entered survey estimation (roles)

| Role | Mechanism | Representative precedents | Design-based guarantee? |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Frame coding (productivity) | text → NAICS/NTEE code → stratification variable | StatCan G-Code/ML on BN "MBA" text; Census SS-4 autocoder and ML; ABS Autocoder; Ma (2021) NTEE | n/a. It changes the stratifier, not the estimator. |
| B. Unit-level auxiliary via predicted y | classifier on web text, trained on the survey sample → ŷ for all frame units → model-assisted, calibration or difference estimator | ISTAT ICT (Barcaroli, Scannapieco, Righi, Bianchi et al.); Beręsewicz et al. (2021) OJA model-assisted calibration | Yes, if the estimator is model-assisted (GREG, model calibration or difference); bias protection regardless of model quality. |
| C. Area-level covariate in SAE | image/phone/text features aggregated to areas → Fay–Herriot / nested-error / EBP / ML | BHF (1988) LANDSAT; NASS CDL; World Bank satellite SAE; mobile phone (Schmid et al. 2017); Google Trends (Porter et al. 2014); Wikipedia (Sheehan et al. 2019) | No. Model-based, so the covariate's measurement error and representativeness matter (Marchetti et al. 2015). |
| D. Model-based replacement of the survey | predict y for all units and aggregate | CBS innovative firms from websites; ZEW/Kinne & Lenz; StatCan model-based crop yields | No. This is where bias risk is highest. |
| E. Imputation / mass imputation | text or LLM features → impute item or unit nonresponse | NCSES BERD uses company websites and 10-Ks for substitution; Bank of England LLM hidden states; Kim & Lee (2023) | Depends on the MAR assumption plus the model. |
| F. Hybrid human+model estimation | ML/LLM prediction for all units + labelled probability subsample → PPI = difference estimator | Ye & Yoganarasimhan (2026); PPI-IPW (2025); Dagdoug & Haziza (2026) | Yes (difference/GREG form). |

The user's proposal ("embeddings as covariates for stratification, nonresponse, calibration, SAE") sits mostly in B, C and F. **B with raw embeddings, stratification on embedding clusters, and embedding-based response propensity have no direct published precedent that I could find.**

## 2. Precedents in enterprise and business statistics

### 2.1 ISTAT — ICT usage and e-commerce in enterprises (the anchor precedent)

- **Barcaroli, G., Nurra, A., Salamone, S., Scannapieco, M., Scarnò, M., Summa, D. (2015).** "Internet as Data Source in the Istat Survey on ICT in Enterprises." *Austrian Journal of Statistics* 44(2), 31–43. https://www.ajs.or.at/index.php/ajs/article/view/vol44-2-3 [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked]
  - Population: enterprises with 10+ persons employed in industry and services. Pilot: about 8,600 websites from about 19,000 responding enterprises. The target was "systematic scraping of the near 100,000 websites" of the whole population.
- **Barcaroli, G., Scannapieco, M., Summa, D. (2016).** "On the use of internet as a data source for official statistics: a strategy for identifying enterprises on the web." *Rivista Italiana di Economia Demografia e Statistica* (RIEDS) 2016. https://www.istat.it/it/files/2018/06/a4_RIEDS-2016.pdf [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked]
  - Coverage arithmetic: about 200,000 enterprises, about 70% with a website (about 130,000 sites), about 105,000 URLs identified, so URL coverage is about 81%.
- **ISTAT paper to DGINS 2018**, "ICT survey and web scraped data from enterprise websites" (author not confirmed). https://dgins2018.statisticsevents.ro/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/04-IT-data-integration-challenges-DGINS-paper-Istat_Italy.pdf [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked]
  - 2017 figures: about 183,000 enterprises, about 75% with a website (about 135,000). URLs were found for about 101,000, or 75% of the estimated total: 14k from the current survey, 28k from automated URL retrieval, and 59k from earlier rounds and other sources.
- **Barcaroli, G., Bianchi, G., et al. (2016).** "Machine learning and statistical inference: the case of Istat survey on ICT." Italian Statistical Society conference. https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/901e286e98d905768317a3bde45d549754d6b643 [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked]
- **Barcaroli, G., Scannapieco, M. (2019).** "Integration of ICT survey data and Internet data from enterprises websites at the Italian National Institute of Statistics." *Statistical Journal of the IAOS*. DOI 10.3233/SJI-190553. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/SJI-190553 [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked]
  - The survey sample is the **training set**. Models are fitted where both survey answers and web-derived terms exist, then applied to every scraped website.
- **Bianchi, G., Barcaroli, G., Righi, P., Rinaldi, M. (2019).** "Producing contingency table estimates integrating survey data and Big Data." ITACOSM 2019 slides. https://www.istat.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/ITACOSM2019-ICT-Righi.pdf [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked]
  - 2017 data, about 21,000 respondents, 66% response rate. A **model-assisted calibration estimator** for counts of enterprises and persons employed by domain.
- **ISTAT Technical Report (ICT 2018).** https://www.istat.it/it/files//2020/05/Tech_Report_ICT2018.pdf [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked]
  - The production estimator is a calibration estimator on enterprise counts and employment by activity × size class × region. The report introduces **unbiased difference estimators** that use web-predicted values, with sales through web channels as one target.
- **ISTAT experimental statistic: "Estimates of modalities of use of websites by enterprises. Year 2018"** (published 8 May 2020). https://www.istat.it/en/experimental-statistic/estimates-of-modalities-of-use-of-websites-by-enterprises-year-2018/ [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked]
  - Pipeline: URL acquisition → linkage to the SBR → scraping, text mining and ML → inference from scraped enterprises to the target population. The 2018 edition used **"a modified estimator to reduce the selection bias related to the Big Data source"** compared with 2017.
- Righi, P., Barcaroli, G., Golini, N. (2018). Quality evaluation of experimental big-data statistics, European Conference on Quality in Official Statistics (Q2018), Kraków. Title and text were not retrieved; existence comes from reference lists only. [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked]

**What carries over to our project.** (i) Targets were **web-observable y**: web ordering, job-vacancy advertising, social-media links. (ii) Auxiliary = **task-specific predicted y** (one classifier per target), not a generic embedding. (iii) Estimator = calibration, model-assisted or difference, so bias protection holds. (iv) Combined estimates were mostly not significantly different from survey-only estimates. Precision gains were real but small, and no headline relative-efficiency figure appeared in what I could retrieve. (v) The **unit-without-URL stratum** is the main bias source, and it required an explicit fix.

### 2.2 Eurostat — ESSnet Big Data I/II → Web Intelligence Network → Web Intelligence Hub (OBEC)

- ESSnet Big Data I (2016–2018) had WP2, "Web scraping of enterprise web sites", with six principal use cases. ESSnet Big Data II had WPC, "Enterprise Characteristics", and produced a **reference methodological framework for OBEC (Deliverable C6)**. Source: CROS portal pages https://cros.ec.europa.eu/book-page/issue-08-moving-big-data-statistical-production and https://cros.ec.europa.eu/node/523 [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked]
- **ESSnet Trusted Smart Statistics – Web Intelligence Network (WIN)** ran April 2021–March 2025, with 17 organisations from 14 countries, led by Statistics Poland. WP2 use cases are OJA (online job ads) and **OBEC (Online Based Enterprise Characteristics)**. https://cros.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2023-12/d1.1_wiser_group_inception_report.pdf ; https://cros.ec.europa.eu/book-page/web-intelligence-hub [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked]
- WIN, "URL finding methodology" (31 Jan 2022), joint work of WP2 and WP3 UC5. It covers search-engine queries, page scraping, feature extraction and a classifier for "is this the enterprise's site". It argues that scraped sites can supplement the ICT survey, which "struggles with low response rates". https://cros.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2023-12/20220131_url_finding_methodology.pdf [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked]
- The OBEC population is enterprises with 10+ employees. Statistics Poland used online business registers to look up URLs. Production status of OBEC in the WIH was not confirmed; the sources say "implementation of the production process is on the way". [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked]

**Relevance.** This is the institutional template for "web text for every frame unit". Its documented hard problems (URL finding, linkage, coverage) apply directly to us, before embeddings even enter.

### 2.3 Statistics Netherlands (CBS) and ZEW — innovation from websites (Role D)

- **Daas, P.J.H., van der Doef, S. (2020).** "Detecting innovative companies via their website." *Statistical Journal of the IAOS* 36(4), 1239–1251. DOI 10.3233/SJI-200627. [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked] Working-paper version: CBS CBDS WP 01-21, "Using website texts to detect innovative companies". https://www.cbs.nl/-/media/innovatie/using-website-texts-todetect-innovative-companies.pdf
  - Model trained on website text of firms in the Dutch CIS (10+ persons). It reproduced CIS results and detected innovative firms **below 10 employees** (start-ups). A later summary reports about 93% maximum accuracy against survey results. [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked]
  - Self-reported issues: **model stability, model bias, minimum number of words extracted, companies without a website**.
  - Later a CBS beta product ("experimental statistics") on innovation in about 500,000 SMEs: https://unstats.un.org/bigdata/events/2024/transport-seminar/presentations/Session%202%20-%20Netherlands%20-%20Web%20scraping%20for%20business%20statistics.pdf [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked]
- CBS × Dataprovider (2019): a file of all Dutch corporate websites linked to the General Business Register (ABR) found about 50,000 companies with mostly online turnover. https://www.cbs.nl/en-gb/corporate/2019/20/cbs-and-dataprovider-successfully-map-internet-economy [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked]
- **Kinne, J., Lenz, D. (2021).** "Predicting innovative firms using web mining and deep learning." *PLoS ONE* 16(4): e0249071. DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0249071. The title is CONFIRMED by the ARGUS repository README (`datawizard1337/ARGUS`), which links the ZEW Discussion Paper 19-001 version. The PLoS ONE volume, article number and DOI are [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked]. Labels come from the Mannheim Innovation Panel; a DNN on website text predicts product-innovator status.
- **Kinne, J., Axenbeck, J. (2018).** "Web mining of firm websites: a framework for web scraping and a pilot study for Germany." ZEW Discussion Paper 18-033. https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/46518 . [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked] Website use and characteristics differ by firm size, age, location and sector, and low broadband seems to keep some firms offline.

**Relevance.** These show the predictive signal exists for innovation-type y. They are model-based replacements, though, so they do not show design-based variance reduction. They also make the selection-on-website-presence problem explicit.

The same registration opens GST/HST, payroll and import/export program accounts. The older RC1C example: "Construction – Installing residential hardwood flooring", described with "at least one noun, a verb, and an adjective". https://www.cchwebsites.com/content/pdf/tax_forms/ca/en/rc1c_en.pdf [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked]

That is a precise, novel and realistic test.

### 3.2 Poverty mapping with satellites (World Bank / academic)
- **Jean, N., Burke, M., Xie, M., Davis, W.M., Lobell, D.B., Ermon, S. (2016).** "Combining satellite imagery and machine learning to predict poverty." *Science* 353(6301), 790–794. DOI 10.1126/science.aaf7894. A CNN on imagery explains up to 75% of variation in local outcomes in five African countries. The title and year are CONFIRMED by the `nealjean/predicting-poverty` README. Its data list covers Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda, Nigeria and Malawi, which is consistent with "five African countries". The *Science* volume, pages, DOI and the 75% figure are [UNVERIFIED: not re-checked].
- **Engstrom, R., Hersh, J., Newhouse, D. ## 4. Red-team lessons distilled from the precedents

| Lesson | Evidence | Implication for our simulation |
|---|---|---|
| The auxiliary is missing for small units, and smallness predicts y | Eurostat 2025: 76.67% small vs 95.65% large; Italy micro 64% vs big 83%; ABS 2008–09: 31% (<5 employees) vs 95% (200+); our 990 check: about 45% vs 88% | Simulate **realistic, size-correlated missingness** of web text. Report gains separately for the "has text" and "no text" strata. The estimator must handle a missing auxiliary, for example with a missingness indicator or a separate calibration group. |
| URL linkage is imperfect | ISTAT: 75–81% of existing websites found; WIN URL-finding methodology | Add **linkage error** (wrong-site text) as a stress scenario. |
| Gains concentrate on web-observable y | ISTAT targets are website functionalities; Brazil e-commerce mismatch; CBS innovation | Pick y's where text *should* help (program type, service mix) **and** y's where it should not (revenue given size), as a negative control. |
| Validating against direct estimates misleads | Corral, Henderson & Segovia (2025) | Use a **pseudo-census + repeated sampling** protocol only, with truth known for all units. |
| Non-sampled areas are worse | Newhouse (2023) | For SAE, report sampled and non-sampled domains separately. |
| Covariate measurement error and representativeness | Marchetti et al. (2015); Yelp accuracy higher in rich ZIPs | Check whether embedding-model error is heterogeneous across domains, which would create domain-level bias in SAE. |
| Selection bias needs a dedicated estimator fix | ISTAT 2018 "modified estimator" | Compare HT, GREG, model calibration and cross-fitted ML-assisted estimators. Never use pure model-based aggregation as the "embedding" arm without a design-consistent comparator. |
| Strong conventional auxiliaries already exist | StatCan tax replacement since the 1960s; tax data as auxiliary in the Monthly Food Services Survey | The baseline must include **admin financials when they exist at design time** (e.g. prior-year revenue), or the gains will be inflated. |
| High-dimensional p | Goga (2025); Caughey & Hartman (2017); Porter et al. (2014) | Do not put 1,536 raw dimensions into GREG calibration. Use (a) model calibration on a cross-fitted ŷ(embedding), (b) PCA or PLS to k ≤ 10–20, or (c) ridge/lasso-GREG. Report the weight distribution (negative weights, CV of weights). |

## 6. Implications for the setup (for the benchmark-design note)

1. **Realistic test 1 (frame-time text):** embed prior-year 990 mission plus program text, or BN-style short self-descriptions. Use them for **stratification and model calibration** in the current-year survey. Truth = current-year 990 values. This mirrors CRA BN/T3010 text held at StatCan. Embedding *current-year* Schedule O alongside current-year y would be leakage, because Schedule O can quote financial amounts.
2. **Realistic test 2 (est-time web text):** website text available only for linked units, with size-graded missingness and linkage error, as in ISTAT and OBEC. Requires crawling, which the sandbox mostly cannot do. The XML website field supports simulating the missingness pattern even without content.
3. **Baselines** must include NTEE×state×revenue-class stratification, GREG on prior-year revenue and assets, and a "TF-IDF/LSA ŷ" arm. The question is whether *generic* embeddings beat *cheap* text features, not only whether they beat no text.
4. **Estimators:** HT, Hájek, post-stratified, GREG (conventional x), model calibration (Wu–Sitter) on cross-fitted ŷ from embeddings (An–Haziza–Dagdoug–Tillé), ridge-GREG on k PCs, and PPI/difference. Pure model-based prediction is reported only as a contrast.
5. **Multimodal (Cohere embed-v4)** is worth testing only with a PDF-native population. The UK Charity Commission accounts PDFs and Companies House accounts are the two real candidates, but neither is reachable from the sandbox. **Corrected:** Cohere's own documentation was read from its GitHub documentation repository (`cohere-ai/cohere-developer-experience`) in this session. It CONFIRMS the following for embed-v4.0:
   - **Inputs:** text, images, and mixed text/image inputs ("i.e. PDFs"); an input "can contain a mix of text and image components".
   - **Context:** 128k.
   - **Output dimensions:** 256/512/1024/1536 (default 1536).
   - **API limits:** up to 96 inputs per call and 20 MB of images per request.

   The same docs list newer `embed-v5.0-pro` and `embed-v5.0-fast` models (text, images and mixed; dimensions up to 2048), so embed-v4 is no longer Cohere's latest model. The price of about $0.12 per 1M input text tokens is corroborated only by LiteLLM's third-party price table, which gives the same price for the Cohere API, Bedrock, OCI and Azure. Image-input pricing was not found.
